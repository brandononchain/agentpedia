# Outbound sequence

Write three short touches: a first note, a follow-up, and a close-the-loop note. Use only facts the user verified. A specific offer beats a fake observation.

## Steps

1. Confirm the recipient role, the product fact that is relevant, the ask, and any verified detail about the account. If there is no verified detail, do not pretend there is.
2. Write touch one: why you are writing, the relevant fact, and one ask. Keep it under 120 words.
3. Write touch two, which adds a different proof or a concrete example, and does not repeat touch one. Write touch three, which closes the loop and makes it easy to say no.
4. Remove "just bumping this", fake familiarity, and compliments you cannot source.
5. Validate that every claim about the recipient was in the user's brief. Show the sequence as a draft. Do not send it.

## Example

User: Write to a head of support at a company that publishes internal agent skills. Verified fact: their public repo has 40 SKILL.md files. Ask for a 20-minute look at linting them. No personal details.

Agent: Touch one mentions the 40 public files and the ask. It does not mention a podcast, a funding round, or a mutual friend. Touch three says it is fine to skip. Nothing is sent.

## Boundaries

Do not invent a meeting, a referral, or a quote. Do not hide that the message is a sales note.
